THE REUSABLE PROMPT SKELETON
============================
Role · Objective · Context · Examples · Format   (R-O-C-E-F)

Copy this. Fill the five blanks. Delete any line that truly does not
apply -- but make that a decision, not an accident. Most weak prompts
are weak because a blank was left empty and the assistant had to guess.

------------------------------------------------------------
ROLE -- who should the assistant be?

    "You are a __________ who __________."

    A role gives the assistant a voice, a standard, and a point of
    view. "You are a veteran office manager who writes warm,
    plain-English notes to patients" produces different words than
    "You are a corporate lawyer." Pick the colleague you would
    actually hand this task to.

------------------------------------------------------------
OBJECTIVE -- what exactly do you want, and why?

    "Draft / summarize / compare / rewrite __________.
     The goal is that the reader __________."

    Name the ONE outcome that means success. "Write about the new
    portal" is not an objective. "Get patients to feel reassured and
    know the single action they must take" is.

------------------------------------------------------------
CONTEXT -- what would a competent colleague need to know?

    - Audience: __________ (who reads this; what they already know)
    - Facts it cannot guess: __________ (names, dates, numbers, limits)
    - What must be true / must NOT be said: __________
    - Your voice: __________ (formal? warm? plain? our house style?)

    This is the half of the message people forget to send. The
    assistant knows the whole internet and nothing about your Tuesday.

------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLES -- show, do not just tell.

    "Here is one that worked / the tone to match: __________"

    Paste one to three short samples. One good example teaches tone
    faster than a paragraph of adjectives. This is "few-shot"
    prompting: you give it a few shots at the target.

------------------------------------------------------------
FORMAT -- what shape should the answer take?

    - Length: __________
    - Structure: __________ (bullets? subject + body? a table? headings?)
    - Must include / must end with: __________

    If you do not name the shape, you get an essay when you wanted
    three bullet points.

------------------------------------------------------------
AFTER THE FIRST DRAFT -- the real work.

    The follow-up is where the value lives. Read the draft with a cold
    eye, then say what to change:

      "Warmer."   "Half as long."   "Add the March 1 deadline."
      "You invented a discount -- remove it."
      "Keep paragraph 2, redo the rest."

    Two or three rounds of correction beat one heroic opening prompt.
    You are not casting a spell. You are editing a fast, willing,
    sometimes-wrong colleague.

------------------------------------------------------------
REMEMBER: You choose the tool. You own the verdict. The assistant
drafts, generates, and accelerates. You decide what ships, you verify
every fact it states, and you put your name on it. AI is an assistant,
not an authority.
