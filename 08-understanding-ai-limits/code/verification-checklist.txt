====================================================================
THE VERIFICATION CHECKLIST  —  your fact-check playbook
====================================================================
Print this. Adapt it. Keep it next to your keyboard for the rest of
the course. It is the tool the whole week is teaching you to build.

Core rule:  AI DRAFTS. YOU VERIFY. YOU OWN THE VERDICT.
            AI is an assistant, not an authority.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 1 — THE ERROR TAXONOMY  (learn the six tells)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. HALLUCINATED FACT — a confident statement that is simply false.
   Tell: oddly specific, no way to check it, "just knows" it.

2. FABRICATED CITATION — a study, report, DOI, case, or URL that
   does not exist, or a real source that says something else.
   Tell: perfect-looking title + author + year that returns nothing
   when you actually search for it.

3. FAKE QUOTE — real person, words they never said (or a real quote
   bent out of shape).
   Tell: sounds too on-the-nose; no primary recording or transcript.

4. OUTDATED / TRAINING-CUTOFF — true once, stale now; a "latest" or
   "most capable today" claim frozen at the model's cutoff date.
   Tell: names a version/price/law that has since moved.

5. PLAUSIBLE-WRONG NUMBER — a statistic with the right shape and a
   wrong (often inflated, suspiciously round or precise) value.
   Tell: "4.5 hours a day," "73%," no source, too good to be true.

6. BIASED / SKEWED FRAME — a one-sided, stereotyped, or
   confidently-settled take that hides real disagreement.
   Tell: "the picture is now settled," "everyone agrees," no caveats.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2 — THE FIVE VERIFICATION MOVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] CROSS-CHECK. Confirm the claim in at least one independent,
    trusted source you did not get from the AI.
[ ] ASK FOR THE SOURCE — THEN FOLLOW IT. Make the AI cite its claim,
    then open the actual source. A citation you did not open is not a
    citation; it is a decoration.
[ ] CHECK AGAINST A TRUSTED ANCHOR. Compare to a primary source, an
    official page, the vendor's own docs, or a subject-matter expert.
[ ] TRIANGULATE. Ask a second, different AI tool the same question.
    Where they disagree is exactly where to dig. (Agreement is NOT
    proof — two models can share the same wrong training data.)
[ ] THE STAKE-YOUR-NAME TEST. Would I put my name under this, in
    front of my boss / my client / a regulator, and answer for it if
    it is wrong? If not, it is not verified. It is a rumor with
    good grammar.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3 — WHEN *NOT* TO USE AI  (the two-question gate)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Ask before you reach for the tool:
  Q1: How high are the STAKES if this is wrong? (money, health,
      safety, legal, someone's reputation, a decision that can't be
      undone)
  Q2: How EASILY can I VERIFY it against a trusted source?

  HIGH stakes + HARD to verify   -> do NOT rely on AI; use a human
                                    expert / primary source.
  HIGH stakes + EASY to verify   -> AI may draft; you verify every
                                    line before it ships.
  LOW stakes + EASY to verify    -> AI is a great fit.
  LOW stakes + HARD to verify    -> fine for a first draft; label it
                                    unverified.

Never paste confidential info, PII, or PHI into a consumer AI tool.
(See Appendix C.) And never fact-check AI with the same AI — a tool
cannot be its own witness.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 4 — THE CLAIM WORKSHEET  (copy one row per claim you check)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
CLAIM (quote it):
TYPE (1-6 above):
HOW I CHECKED (which trusted source / search / expert):
WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE:
VERDICT ( verified / wrong / can't-verify ):
WOULD I STAKE MY NAME ON IT? ( yes / no ):

--------------------------------------------------------------------
Bottom line: you are not looking for reasons to distrust the tool,
and you are not looking for reasons to trust it. You are testing it.
Trust is earned one verified claim at a time.
