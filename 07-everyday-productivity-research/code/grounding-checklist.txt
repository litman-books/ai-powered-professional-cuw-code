SUMMARY GROUNDING CHECKLIST
A worksheet for checking an AI summary against its source, sentence by sentence.
Chapter 7 — The AI-Powered Professional
================================================================

WHY THIS EXISTS
---------------
An AI summary is a claim, not a fact. "Grounding" means proving that each
sentence of the summary is actually supported by the source document — by
finding the exact words in the source that back it up. If you cannot find the
support, the sentence is unsupported, and an unsupported sentence in a summary
is a hallucination wearing a summary's clothes.

You choose the tool. You own the verdict. The AI wrote the summary; YOU decide
what goes in front of your boss.


HOW TO USE THIS SHEET
---------------------
1. Number every sentence in the AI summary (1, 2, 3, ...).
2. For each sentence, open the SOURCE and hunt for the words that support it.
3. Mark the sentence with one verdict:
      SUPPORTED  — the source clearly says this; you found the quote.
      PARTIAL    — partly right, but a number/name/claim is off or overstated.
      UNSUPPORTED— you cannot find it in the source, or the source says otherwise.
4. Paste the SOURCE QUOTE (or "none found") in the evidence column.
5. If PARTIAL or UNSUPPORTED, name the failure type (see the list below).
6. Write the CORRECTED sentence.


THE SIX FAILURE TYPES (name the sin, then fix it)
-------------------------------------------------
[F1] Fabricated number      — a figure that isn't in the source (e.g., wrong %).
[F2] Magnitude/decimal error— right figure, wrong scale ($2.1M read as $21M).
[F3] Wrong name/identifier  — a program, person, date, or place renamed.
[F4] Fabricated citation    — a source, study, or author the document never names.
[F5] Added claim / opposite — a recommendation or conclusion the source never made
                              (or the reverse of what it made).
[F6] Overstated causation   — turns "moved alongside" into "was caused by."

TWO RULES BEFORE YOU SIGN
-------------------------
1. "Sounds right" is not evidence. If you cannot point to the words in the
   source, the verdict is UNSUPPORTED — no matter how reasonable it sounds.
2. A single fabricated number or citation poisons the whole summary's
   credibility. Find it, fix it, and note it, or do not send the summary.
