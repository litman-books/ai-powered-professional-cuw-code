----------------------------------------------------------------
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Two years after adopting a hybrid work model, the Meridian Group set out to
answer a simple question: is it working? This report draws on an all-employee
survey, badge-access data from our four regional offices, and voluntary
attrition records to give leadership an honest picture heading into fiscal
year 2026.

The short version: hybrid work is popular, productivity is holding or
improving on most measures, and our office space is meaningfully
underused on Mondays and Fridays. The report closes with four
recommendations, the largest of which — a study of whether to consolidate
from four offices to three — carries the biggest financial stakes and the
most human sensitivity.


----------------------------------------------------------------
2. HOW WE GATHERED THE DATA
----------------------------------------------------------------

The employee survey was fielded from March 3 to March 28, 2025, to all 1,240
employees across our four regional offices in Denver, Austin, Atlanta, and
Portland. We received 1,017 completed responses, a response rate of 82
percent — high enough that we treat the findings as representative, while
noting that the most disengaged employees are the least likely to answer a
survey.

Office-use figures come from badge-access logs for the twelve weeks from
January through March 2025. Attrition figures come from HR records for
calendar years 2023 and 2024. Where we cite an outside benchmark, it is the
Workspace Futures 2024 survey published by Harbor Analytics (a fictional
research firm, sample size 3,000 knowledge workers), which we reference once,
in Section 5.


----------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT EMPLOYEES TOLD US
----------------------------------------------------------------

Preference for hybrid is strong but not universal. Seventy-one percent of
respondents said they prefer a hybrid schedule to either fully remote or
fully in-office work. Of those who prefer hybrid, most chose two to three
in-office days per week as their ideal; very few wanted a five-day return,
and a smaller but vocal group (roughly one in six) said they would prefer to
work fully remote.

Self-reported productivity is up on the measures employees control. On a
five-point scale, satisfaction with the ability to do focused work scored 4.2
out of 5 — the highest of any category we asked about. Employees credited
quiet home mornings and fewer hallway interruptions.

Meetings are the sore spot. Employees reported spending, on average, 17 hours
per week in meetings. When asked what fraction of those meetings "could have
been an email or a short written update," the median answer was 40 percent.
Meeting fatigue was the single most common write-in complaint.


----------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT THE BADGE DATA SHOWS
----------------------------------------------------------------

People come in for each other, not for the building. Badge-access data shows
a clear midweek peak: Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday run near capacity,
while Monday and Friday occupancy sits at roughly 35 percent. In plain terms,
on the two bookend days of the week we are heating, cooling, cleaning, and
leasing space that is two-thirds empty.

Occupancy also varies by office. Austin and Atlanta run consistently fuller
than Denver and Portland, a gap that widened over the twelve-week window.


----------------------------------------------------------------
5. RETENTION AND THE "FLEXIBLE FRIDAYS" PILOT
----------------------------------------------------------------

In September 2024 we launched a pilot called Flexible Fridays, which let
teams designate Friday as a no-meetings, work-from-anywhere day. It was not
introduced to fix attrition specifically, but the retention numbers moved in
the right direction alongside it.

Voluntary attrition fell from 14 percent in calendar year 2023 to 9 percent
in calendar year 2024. We are careful not to claim the pilot alone caused the
drop — the labor market cooled over the same period, which also reduces
voluntary departures — but exit-interview sentiment about flexibility
improved markedly. For outside context, the Workspace Futures 2024 survey
(Harbor Analytics, n=3,000) reported that flexibility ranked as the second
most common reason knowledge workers gave for staying in a job, behind
compensation.


----------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE REAL-ESTATE QUESTION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Given the Monday/Friday vacancy, leadership has asked People Operations and
Finance to jointly study whether to consolidate from four offices to three by
the end of 2026. A preliminary Finance estimate puts the annual savings from
closing one mid-size office at approximately $2.1 million, before one-time
relocation and lease-exit costs.

This is not a recommendation to close an office. It is a recommendation to
study the option carefully, because it is the single largest cost lever in
this report and because it affects where real people are asked to work.


----------------------------------------------------------------
7. RECOMMENDATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------

1. Standardize "anchor days." Designate Tuesday through Thursday as expected
   in-office days for most teams, and protect Monday and Friday for focused
   and flexible work. This formalizes what employees are already doing.

2. Cut recurring meetings by 20 percent. Audit every standing meeting, kill
   or shorten the weakest, and shift status updates to short written posts.

3. Commission the office-consolidation study. Have Finance and People
   Operations return a recommendation, with numbers, by the end of Q3 2026.

4. Invest in the hybrid setup. Budget approximately $350,000 in FY2026 for
   home-office stipends and collaboration tools, so that the two-thirds of
   the week spent out of the office is well-equipped.


----------------------------------------------------------------
8. RISKS AND WHAT WE DID NOT MEASURE
----------------------------------------------------------------

This report measures preference, self-reported productivity, occupancy, and
attrition. It does NOT measure objective output quality, customer outcomes,
mentorship of junior staff, or the harder-to-see cost of weaker in-person
relationships. Anyone using this report to justify a large decision — an
office closure especially — should treat those unmeasured factors as open
questions, not as settled in hybrid's favor.
